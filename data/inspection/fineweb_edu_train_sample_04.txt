What is a (corporate) power purchase agreement?
A power purchase agreement is a long-term electricity supply agreement between an installation operator (seller) and an electricity customer (buyer). The buyer can be an intermediary energy trader or energy supplier, or a major direct industrial consumer such as an IT company who need renewable power for its data centres. The agreements are generally signed for a period of up to 10 years, though shorter-term PPAs are also possible.
Purchasing power directly through agreements like these is nothing new in the energy sector. It has long been common practice to conclude direct agreements with energy-intensive customers who want long-term price certainty. In principle, the agreements can cover all types of energy supply, including electricity generated by fossil fuels. However, in today’s energy world, corporate power purchase agreements are understood as the supply of power generated by renewable energy from a wind turbine, photovoltaic installation, biomass plant or hydroelectric plant to an industrial end consumer.
The generator of the renewable energy receives a fixed price per megawatt hour, meaning that it can expect fixed returns on its investment and offer the bank the certainty it requires for the loans. The high-demand customer can therefore ensure that its renewable energy supply comes either directly from a specific plant, or from a green portfolio, at a fixed price for the duration of the agreement. The proof of the green quality and origin of the energy supply is provided by the guarantees of origin (GO) of the energy-generating plants.
Why are PPAs in vogue?
There are currently two developments taking place on the market that are reinforcing each other.
On the one hand, subsidies for renewable energies in Europe are falling or are due to be phased out in the near future, particularly in offshore wind. This trend is set to continue. Without subsidies, operators of renewable energy plants must find ways of hedging at least part of their investment risk. One approach is to sell future generation to a buyer.
On the other hand, companies are increasingly looking for energy solutions to meet their ambitious sustainability targets, and are increasingly demanding a direct supply of green power. Almost half of Fortune 500 companies had already set climate targets by 2017. While these primarily included global IT companies with a large energy demand, like Facebook and Microsoft, there is now a growing interest from all sectors.
What types of PPAs are there?
PPAs differ according to who the owner of the electricity generation is and who their customer is – as well as according to the type of power transmission.
The developer and operator of the wind or solar farm sells the electricity directly to a high-demand customer. This ensures stable prices and access to green energy. The seller is not dependent on subsidies, has a lower market risk and secures stable revenues in the long term. For example, in autumn 2017, Vattenfall and Microsoft concluded a 10-year agreement in the Netherlands for the provision of green energy from the new Wieringermeer wind farm, which is located right next to the data centre.

The electricity is purchased and sold by a trader who usually offers other services, such as for balancing power, forecasts or optimisation of electricity generation. There are also PPAs involving an electricity supply which is virtual rather than physical. Here, the green electricity is traded on the electricity exchange. In this type of agreement, the market risk is hedged and the electricity supply is covered by guarantees of origin (GO).

Understanding Treatment for Psychosis
Psychosis is a psychiatric condition in which a person loses touch with his or her sense of reality. People with psychosis may experience hallucinations, delusions, or disorganized thinking; they may hear voices no one else can hear or perceive threats that do not actually exist. Additionally, their thoughts, feelings, and behaviors may be extremely inappropriate and disconnected from the world around them, without the individual being aware of this disconnection. Psychosis is also often associated with a number of mental health disorders such as schizophrenia, Alzheimer’s disease, or substance abuse. While psychosis may only last a few days or weeks, in some instances it can be a chronic condition. The presence of psychosis is the sign of a serious illness and anyone displaying symptoms of psychosis should be evaluated immediately in order to receive appropriate medical treatment.

What Are Functional Groups in Chemistry?
In chemistry, functional groups are specific subunits of larger chemical compounds that are responsible for that compound’s bonding behavior. One can think of functional groups as recurring patterns of atoms that play consistent functional roles (bonding and properties) across the molecules they show up in. Functional groups tend to have similar bonding behavior, no matter which molecule they show up in, so functional groups can be used to predict chemical reactions and the behavior of compounds that contain a functional group. The presence of functional groups can also be used to predict the physical properties of an organic compound. A molecule’s functional groups affect its structure, reactivity, bonding behavior, and solubility,
The simplest example of a functional group is the hydroxyl group. A hydroxyl group simple has a chemical formula of OH. Many organic and inorganic compounds contain a hydroxyl group, including water (H2O) and most acids. Hydroxyl groups tend to act as a “cap” on chains of organic molecules that can be removed to let them bond with other functional groups. For instance, fatty acids are a lipid that is formed by a chain of hydrocarbons attached to a hydroxyl group. The hydroxyl groups are removed from fatty acid bases when forming triglycerides. The removal of a hydroxyl group allows the fatty acid to bond with the glycerol backbone. When fatty acids are removed from a triglyceride, a hydroxyl group is reattached to the end.
Compounds that contain hydroxyl groups tend to form acids in aqueous solutions. The polarity of the hydroxyl group allows the hydrogen to be removed easily, leaving behind a hydroxyl anion. This polarity also allows hydroxyl compounds to be soluble in water. The presence of a polar hydrogen end allows compounds with hydroxyl groups to engage in hydrogen bonding. As such, hydroxyl compounds tend to have higher boiling and evaporating points than structurally similar molecules that lack a hydroxyl group.
The presence of functional groups explains the maxim in chemistry that “like dissolves like.” The mutual solubility of compounds is due to the interaction of shared functional groups. Water readily dissolves sugar because both sugar and water contain a hydroxyl group and hydroxyl groups interact strongly with each other. If a functional group is more electronegative than its base then the functional group becomes polar and makes the molecule soluble in aqueous solutions.
Functional groups themselves can also contain functional groups. The carboxyl group is a perfect example. The carboxyl group itself contains two functional groups: a carbonyl group formed by a double bonded carbon and oxygen (C=O), and a hydroxyl group formed from a single bonded oxygen and a hydrogen (O–H). The presence of these two functional groups explains the bonding behavior of carboxyl groups.
Carboxyl As A Functional Group
As stated before, carboxyl is a functional group with a formula of R[COOH]. Carboxyl groups are ubiquitous in biological systems because they give organic compounds the polar and solvent properties that are necessary for life.
Carboxyl is extremely polar due to the presence of a double bond between the oxygen and carbon in the carbonyl base. As a result, the attachment of a carboxyl group makes a compound very soluble in other polar solvents. This polarity, coupled with the hydroxyl group at the other end of the molecule, allows carboxyl compounds to engage in a strong form of hydrogen bonding. The highly polar carbonyl base attracts the hydroxyl base of nearby molecules. Because hydrogen is so small, the hydroxyl base can get very close to the carbonyl base, which creates a strong electrostatic attraction.
As a consequence of this hydrogen bonding, carboxyl compounds tend to have very high melting and boiling points. For instance, both aspartate and glutamate have carboxyl groups in their side chains. The hydrogen bonding between carboxyl groups stabilizes the structure of the proteins. Carboxyl groups also tend to facilitate the linking of larger macromolecules. Carboxyl groups are also crucial for the formation of peptide bonds. Peptide bond form when the amino group of an amino acid bonds to the carbonyl group of a carboxyl group.
Examples Of Compounds With Carboxyl Groups
Formic Acid (HCOOH): The simplest possible carboxylic acid. Formic acid is what causes the pain from bee stings and ant bites.
Acetic Acid (CH3COOH): Acetic acid forms from the joining of a carboxyl and a methyl group (CH3). It is known for its sour taste and has wide-spread uses in commercial and textile industries.
Citric Acid (C6H8O7): Citric acid is a weak acid that is most commonly associated with sour fruits, like lemons or lime. Citric acid actually has 3 individual carboxyl groups. Citric acid is used by all aerobic organisms and is an important element of the Krebs cycle—the process by which ATP is produced from the oxidation of acetyl-CoA.
Butyric Acid (C3H7COOH): Butyric acid is a carboxylic acid found in esters of animal fats and plant oils. Butyric acid is the chemical that is responsible for the characteristic “buttery” taste of butter.
Oxalic Acid (HOOCCOOH): Oxalic acid is formed by the conjunction of two carboxyl groups via their central carbon atoms. It is the simplest possible dicarboxylic acid. In humans, it is most commonly found in the form of calcium oxalate, the main component of kidney stones.
Benzoic Acid (C6H5COOH): Benzoic acid comes mostly from plants is used in food preservation and medical technologies. Benzoic acid inhibits the growth of fungi, molds, and bacteria, so it has historically been used as a means of preserving perishable good. For the same reason, benzoic acid has found some use in the medical field as a treatment for fungal skin infections. Benzoic acid is also an important precursor to compound that make up insect repellents and certain artificial flavors.
Fatty Acids: Fatty acids are biological lipids that are made out of a carboxyl base attached to a linear hydrocarbon chain. Fatty acids are the simplest kind of lipid and are the building block for more complex lipids and lipo-proteins. The unique combination of the hydrophilic carboxyl head and hydrophobic hydrocarbon chain allows them to bond to glycerides. Fatty acids are an important source of energy for cellular respiration.
Other Kinds of Functional Groups
There are many other kinds of functional groups, each characterized by their distinct bonding properties. Some examples include:
Alkyls: R[CH2]: Alkyls are the base constituents of most single-bonded hydrocarbons, like methane, propane, ethane, octane, etc. Compounds made form alkyls tend to readily combust.
